## 1. The strategic problem

Foodyzz launches into a category with three funded incumbents, zero social following, zero reviews, zero traffic, and no iOS app live yet. It cannot win on scale, trust signals, or ad budget on day one.

But the category has an unusual weakness: **every competitor advertises a price that is not the price.** Zoomo shows "$20/week" on a page where the standard plan is $59/week. Whizz shows "$169/mo." Nobody publishes a checkout total. And the customer is a rider working 17 hours a week for $366, who has been burned before — 42% report being underpaid by the apps they work for.

Meanwhile the biggest story in this rider's life right now is not price. It is **risk**: 54% have had a bike stolen, 27,000 vehicles were seized in 2024 for being the wrong classification, and battery fires are up 70% this year.

So the position is not "cheapest." It is **the one you can actually trust with the money and the risk.**

## 2. Positioning statement

**For** New York City delivery riders who need a bike that will not be seized, will not burn, and will not cost them a month's earnings up front,
**Foodyzz** is a UL-certified e-bike you can rent, own in twelve months, or buy outright,
**delivered to your door** with no credit check and no store visit,
**because** we publish the full price before you tap pay, we charge you only when the bike is in your hands, and the certification mark is on the frame.

**Unlike** Whizz, Zoomo and JOCO, Foodyzz shows the checkout total up front and gets a rider to ownership in twelve months for less than half of what rent-to-own costs anywhere else in this city.

## 3. The five pillars

Every asset should ladder to one of these. If a post ladders to none, it does not ship.

### Pillar 1 — **Own it in 12 months** (the commercial weapon)

The strongest verifiable number Foodyzz has.

- **$75.98/month × 12 months = $911.76, and the bike is yours.**
- Whizz advertises rent-to-own from **$169/month for 12 months ≈ $2,028**.
- That is **~55% less over the same twelve months.** Do not claim a shorter term — ours is 12, the same as theirs. The claim is money, not speed.
- Pay off early any time, no penalty.

**Honesty guardrail:** Whizz's $169 includes unlimited battery swaps; ours does not. Any comparison graphic shows both columns. Being scrupulously fair here is not a compliance chore — it is the proof of the whole positioning.

### Pillar 2 — **Certified, and the mark is on the frame** (the trust weapon)

- New York's Local Law 39 (effective Sept 16, 2023) bars the **sale, lease or rental** of any e-bike or battery in NYC that is not UL certified.
- Foodyzz bikes: **UL 2849** electrical system (cert. **CU 726061660001**), **UL 2271** battery (cert. **CU 72303450 0003**), both **tested by TÜV Rheinland**, mark displayed on the frame.
- **The certificate numbers are public.** Anyone can look them up on certipedia.com — which turns "trust us" into "check us," and that is a far stronger move for a brand with no reviews.
- FDNY battery fires are up ~**70% in 2026** (235 YTD vs 138) — while **deaths fell to 1 in 2025 from 18 in 2023**, credited to the certification mandate.
- **Class 2, limited to 15 mph** — throttle-capable, and configured to New York City's citywide e-bike limit in force since **Oct 24, 2025**. Well inside the e-bike definition, so no registration, licence or plate. **Competitors renting 20–25 mph bikes into this city have a problem they have not solved.**
- IP65. Carries 300 lbs rider + cargo. Integrated BMS.

**Tone guardrail:** this is not a fear campaign. The story is that certification is *working*, and this is how a rider gets on the right side of that number. Never show fire imagery. Never imply a competitor's bikes are unsafe.

**Wording guardrail:** say **"UL 2849 certified, tested by TÜV Rheinland."** Never "UL Listed" — that implies UL Solutions did the listing, which is a different thing and exactly the kind of detail that gets picked apart by a competitor who publishes comparison posts.

**The under-used asset here is the throttle.** Class 2 means throttle assist pulling away from a light with a loaded bag — the thing a delivery rider actually feels — without crossing into moped territory. It appears in no Foodyzz marketing today and in no competitor's either.

### Pillar 3 — **The price you see is the price you pay** (the differentiator)

- Publish the full table: base rate, the $5.99 bundle, the deposit, tax and card fee.
- **$95.95 for four weeks on Model 1.** Say the real number.
- Fees are charged **once per rental period, not per week** — so a longer term costs a little less per week. 4 weeks = $23.99/wk; 12 weeks = $22.99/wk. **A dollar. Mention it, don't build on it.**
- Card is authorised at booking, **charged only at delivery**. Cancel free before then.
- Deposit is **$100, charged at delivery as a separate transaction and refunded when the bike comes back**, minus damage — and damage can never exceed the deposit. It is charged, not held; say it that way.
- **The GPS tracker is optional and off by default.** It used to be pre-ticked. "We don't tick boxes for you" is a specific, checkable trust line almost nobody in this category can say.
- **The all-in total is on the checkout screen** before you continue — the app used to list the parts without ever summing them.

This pillar is the one nobody can copy quickly, because copying it means competitors admitting their own headline prices are not real.

### Pillar 5 — **What happens when it goes wrong** (the retention weapon)

New as of Aug 7, and nobody else in this category talks about any of it.

- **Every rental includes the Protection Plan** — a damage and theft waiver on the bike, in the rate, at no separate charge. It waives 50% of eligible repair and damage, and caps liability for a stolen bike at $500 on conditions.
- **It is not insurance.** Never use that word, or "coverage". It waives. It applies to the bike, never to the rider — no injuries, no lost earnings, no third-party claims. It does **not** apply to Rent to Buy.
- **On Rent to Buy, if your income drops, your payment can drop.** Once half the total is paid, a rider whose income falls 25%+ through involuntary job loss, reduced work, illness, pregnancy or disability can have each payment cut by that percentage or 50%, whichever is less — **and the total to own does not increase**, the term extends. This is a legal right under NY rental-purchase law, not a Foodyzz favour, and the copy must not present it as generosity. For a courier deciding whether to commit to twelve months, it answers the exact fear that stops them.
- **A helmet is required on every ride** — ticked at checkout, stricter than the law. **Foodyzz does not supply one.** Say both halves.

**Guardrail:** every one of these is a conditional promise. State the conditions in the same breath, or don't make the claim. The whole brand is "we tell you the real number" — a waiver described without its exclusions is the same sin as a headline price without its minimum term.

### Pillar 4 — **No credit check, no store visit, brought to your door** (the access weapon)

- Phone number and a code. No credit check anywhere in the flow.
- ID needed for rentals: driver licence front and back, plus a utility bill, bank statement or lease.
- **Buying skips the document check entirely.**
- We bring the bike to the address on the account, 5–9 PM, any day in the next two weeks.
- Roughly **half of NYC's delivery workforce has been on the job under a year** — this audience is constantly new, constantly needing a first bike, and often has no US credit history.

**Honesty guardrail:** never say "no documents." Rentals need a licence and proof of address. Say what is needed and why — it is a smaller ask than a credit application and that is the point.

## 5. Voice

The audience: ~70,000–80,000 people, ~80% immigrant, working an average 17.4 hours a week for $366.51, half of them less than a year into the job, roughly half having had a bike stolen. They are being marketed to constantly and lied to often.

**Write like this**
- Lead with the number. "$911.76. Then it's yours."
- Short sentences. Sixth-grade reading level in every language.
- Show the arithmetic. Never round in our own favour.
- Name the catch before they find it. "Four-week minimum. Here's why that's still cheaper."
- Respect the work. These are professionals doing a hard job, not "hustlers" or "gig warriors."

**Never write like this**
- Hype stacks, emoji walls, "🔥 GAME CHANGER 🔥"
- Fake urgency, fake scarcity, fake countdowns
- Poverty tourism or savior framing
- "Passive income," "be your own boss," "unlock your potential"
- Anything implying speed, racing, or beating traffic
- Fear imagery around fires, theft or police

**Tagline candidates** (all defensible):
1. **"Own it in twelve months."** — sharpest, leads with the strongest number
2. **"The price you see is the price you pay."** — leads with trust
3. **"Certified. Delivered. Yours."** — covers all three pillars in three words
4. Existing site line: *"Your e-bike, delivered to your door."* — fine, but says nothing a competitor couldn't

**Recommendation: #1 as the campaign line, #2 as the sub-line.** Together they say the two things nobody else can say.

## 7. Proof assets we have vs. need

**Have (usable today):** the real bike photograph with the parts diagram (trapped in a screenshot — extract it), the itemised checkout screen, the rent-to-buy progress screen, the certification block in-app, the spec sheet, all pricing math, every NYC statistic in the rules file.

**Do not have (blocking):** any customer, any review, any rating, any delivery photo, any rider photo, any founder or team photo, a logomark for profile pictures, the original UL certificates in hand (numbers verified, documents come at contract confirmation), a phone number.

**Note the app-store screenshots show empty states** — "NO PROMOTIONS AVAILABLE NEARBY" and "SERVICE HISTORY (0)". They are honest but weak. Reshoot with seeded demo data before using them in ads.

## 8. Competitive stance

| Competitor | Their strength | Our line |
|---|---|---|
| **Whizz** | 228 blog posts, ~4.5K IG, ES/FR/RU sites, unlimited swaps | "Own it in 12 months for $911.76, not 12 months for $2,028." Fair-play on swaps. |
| **Zoomo** | Scale, Uber Eats partnership, $20/wk headline | "Ours is $95.95 for four weeks and we'll show you the line items." |
| **JOCO** | Free swaps, storage, maintenance; advocacy partnerships | Least price transparency in the market — ask them for a total. |
| **Fly E-Bike** | Storefronts, DOT trade-in programme | "$899 delivered, no store visit, certified." |
| **PopWheels** | Charging network | **Not a competitor — a partner.** They solve charging, we solve the bike. |

**Rule: never name a competitor in an ad or organic post.** Comparison content lives on the blog, where it can be sourced, dated and fair. Whizz publishes comparison posts about everyone; expect one about Foodyzz within weeks of launch, and let the blog be the thing it has to argue with.
