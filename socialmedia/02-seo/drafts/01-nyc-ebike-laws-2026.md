# NYC E-Bike Laws in 2026: What's Actually Legal Right Now

*Last updated: August 13, 2026*

If you deliver by e-bike in New York City, two things changed recently that make most of what you'll find online wrong. The city set a **15 mph speed limit for every e-bike, no matter its class**, in October 2025. And in March 2026 the NYPD stopped issuing criminal summonses for minor cycling violations — though it has kept writing some anyway.

Here is the current picture, with the source for each piece so you can check it yourself.

## The short version

- **15 mph is the legal riding speed in NYC for every e-bike** — Class 1, 2 and 3 alike — on streets, bike lanes, greenways, bridges and park drives. This is not about what your bike can do. It's about what you're allowed to do.
- **You do not need a licence, registration or insurance to ride an e-bike** in New York.
- **You do need all three if your bike is legally a moped** — and a lot of bikes sold to delivery riders are. This is where people lose their bikes.
- **Class 3 e-bikes (25 mph) are legal in New York City**, though the 15 mph riding cap still applies.
- **Minor cycling violations became civil, not criminal, on March 27, 2026** — but enforcement has been inconsistent since.

## The 15 mph cap is the rule that catches people out

New York City published a final rule in the City Record on **September 24, 2025**, effective **October 24, 2025**, setting a citywide **15 mph speed limit** for e-bikes, e-scooters and pedal-assist commercial bicycles.

The part riders miss: **it applies to all classes.** A Class 3 e-bike is built to assist up to 25 mph and is perfectly legal to own in New York City. Riding it at 25 mph on a city street is not legal. The device's rating and the road's limit are two different things.

The limit covers streets, bike lanes, greenways, bridges and park drives.

A cycling club sued the City in **February 2026** over how the limit applies inside Central Park, and that case is still open. The citywide rule remains in force while it plays out.

## The three classes, and what they mean here

New York State defines e-bikes in Vehicle and Traffic Law §121-b:

| Class | How it works | Assist cuts off at |
|---|---|---|
| **Class 1** | Pedal-assist only — the motor helps when you pedal | 20 mph |
| **Class 2** | Throttle-capable — the motor can move the bike without pedalling | 20 mph |
| **Class 3** | Pedal-assist, higher speed | 25 mph |

New York City is specifically authorised for Class 3, which most of the state is not. That's why 25 mph bikes are common here.

**None of this changes the 15 mph riding limit.** Class tells you what you're allowed to own and where. The cap tells you how fast you're allowed to go.

## Where the real risk is: your bike might be a moped

This is the single most expensive mistake a delivery rider can make, and it has nothing to do with speeding tickets.

Many of the fat-tyre, throttle-only bikes marketed to delivery riders can do around **28 mph**. Above the Class 3 threshold, a device stops being an e-bike under New York law and becomes a **moped** — a motor vehicle. Which means it needs:

- DMV registration
- A driver's licence
- Insurance
- A licence plate

Ride one without those and it can be seized. The City seized **more than 27,000 motorized vehicles in 2024**, roughly a 50% increase on the year before, and **over 42,000 since 2022 — more than half of which were destroyed.**

Since **January 7, 2025**, New York State law (S.7703-B / A.8450-B) has required dealers to register mopeds with the DMV at the point of sale. That helps if you buy from a real dealer. It does nothing if you buy from a marketplace listing, which is where most of these bikes come from.

## Licence, registration, insurance, helmet

**For an e-bike (Class 1, 2 or 3):**

- **Licence:** not required
- **Registration:** not required
- **Insurance:** not required today. A State bill (A.4083 / S.2528) would require $25,000 in liability coverage for e-bikes in New York City — **it is not law**, and you should treat anyone telling you otherwise with suspicion.
- **Helmet:** required for Class 3 riders and for riders under 18. If you're delivering commercially, check the current NYC DOT commercial-cycling rules — they carry additional requirements, and they're worth reading once properly rather than trusting a summary.

**For a moped:** licence, registration and insurance, all of them, always.

## What changed with tickets in 2026

In late April 2025 the NYPD began issuing **criminal** summonses for e-bike and moped traffic violations. A criminal summons means a court date and a record — a serious matter for anyone, and a much more serious one for a non-citizen.

On **March 18, 2026**, Mayor Mamdani announced an end to criminal enforcement for minor traffic violations, effective **March 27, 2026**. Low-level violations became civil matters instead.

Then the practice didn't quite match the policy. **Between March 31 and July 13, 2026, the NYPD wrote 257 criminal "reckless riding" summonses.** A separate lawsuit is now seeking to restore criminal enforcement.

So the honest answer to "will I get a criminal summons?" in 2026 is: it's supposed to be civil, and usually is, and sometimes isn't. If you receive one, read the paperwork carefully to see which kind it is, and get advice — [Documented](https://documentedny.com/2025/06/19/ebike-nypd-ticket-summons-court/) publishes guidance for immigrant delivery workers, and legal-aid organisations can help.

*This is not legal advice, and we're not lawyers. It's a summary of published rules with links to the originals.*

## Batteries: the rule that applies to whoever sold you the bike

**Local Law 39 of 2023**, effective **September 16, 2023**, bans the **sale, lease or rental** in New York City of e-bikes, e-scooters or lithium-ion batteries that aren't certified to the relevant UL standard:

- **UL 2849** — the e-bike's electrical system
- **UL 2271** — the lithium-ion battery
- **UL 2272** — e-scooter electrical systems

Note that word: **rental.** Any company renting you an e-bike in this city is subject to this law, not just the shop that sold it.

The reason for the law is in the FDNY numbers. Battery fires went from 44 in 2020 to 104 in 2021 to 220 in 2022. But here's the part that doesn't get reported: **deaths fell from 18 in 2023 to 6 in 2024 to 1 in 2025**, which UL Standards & Engagement credits directly to certification being written into law. Fires are up again in 2026 — **235 through this point in the year against 138 at the same point in 2025, about a 70% increase** — while the death toll has stayed at one.

More fires, far fewer deaths. Certified batteries appear to be doing exactly what they were meant to do.

If you want to check your own bike: look for the UL mark on the frame and on the battery. If there isn't one, the bike shouldn't have been rented or sold to you in New York City.

## What this means if you deliver in NYC

1. **Know your bike's real top speed.** Not the marketing number — the actual one. If it's around 28 mph and throttle-only, you're probably riding an unregistered moped.
2. **Ride to 15 mph.** The cap applies regardless of what your bike is rated for.
3. **Look for the UL mark** on the frame and the battery before you buy or rent anything.
4. **Keep your paperwork** if you're on a moped. Registration, licence, insurance.
5. **Read any summons carefully.** Civil and criminal are different documents with very different consequences.
