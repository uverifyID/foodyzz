# Is Your E-Bike Actually a Moped? The NYC Test That Decides If It Gets Seized

*Last updated: August 14, 2026*

New York City seized **more than 27,000 motorized vehicles in 2024** — about 50% more than the year before. Since 2022 the total is **over 42,000, and more than half were destroyed.**

Most of the people who lost one believed they were riding an e-bike. The seller called it an e-bike. The listing said e-bike. Under New York law it wasn't.

Four questions will tell you where you stand.

## The short version

- If your bike can reach roughly **28 mph**, it's almost certainly a **moped** under New York law, not an e-bike.
- A moped needs **DMV registration, a driver's licence, insurance and a plate.** Without them it can be seized.
- Seized doesn't mean impounded until you pay. **More than half of seized vehicles have been destroyed.**
- The seller calling it an e-bike carries no legal weight whatsoever.
- Since **January 7, 2025**, dealers must register mopeds with the DMV at the point of sale — which helps only if you bought from a dealer.

## The four-question test

### 1. Does it have working pedals you can actually ride it with?

**No pedals, or pedals that are decorative →** it is not a bicycle under New York law. It's a moped or a motorcycle. Stop here.

**Yes →** continue.

### 2. What is its real top speed under motor power?

Not the number on the box. The number it actually reaches.

| Real top speed | What it is |
|---|---|
| Up to **20 mph** | Class 1 (pedal-assist) or Class 2 (throttle) e-bike |
| Up to **25 mph** | Class 3 e-bike — legal to own in New York City |
| Around **28 mph** or above | **Moped.** Not an e-bike. |

That 28 mph figure isn't arbitrary — it's roughly where the bikes most commonly sold to delivery riders top out, and it's above every e-bike class ceiling in the statute.

### 3. How is it powered — and how much power?

Throttle-only operation with a high-wattage motor pushes a device out of the bicycle definition and into the motor-vehicle one, even where the pedals technically exist.

If yours moves under throttle alone with no pedalling at all, and it's fast, treat that as a strong signal.

### 4. Does it carry a UL mark?

Look on the frame and on the battery for a **UL 2849** mark (electrical system) or **UL 2271** mark (battery).

This doesn't decide the moped question by itself — but if there's no UL mark, the bike **should not legally have been sold, leased or rented to you in New York City** under Local Law 39 of 2023, and you're likely dealing with a grey-market import where the specs on the listing mean nothing at all.

## What "moped" costs you

If your device is legally a moped, New York requires:

- **DMV registration** and a licence plate
- **A driver's licence** — a regular one; an e-bike needs none
- **Liability insurance**
- **A helmet**, always

Riding without them exposes you to summonses and to seizure.

**And seizure is not a fine.** The Ghost Car Task Force and NYPD enforcement have taken tens of thousands of vehicles, and the majority of seized vehicles have been destroyed rather than returned. There is no version of this where you pay a few hundred dollars and ride home.

For a rider who paid $1,500 to $2,000 for the bike and depends on it for income, that is catastrophic — and it lands hardest on people who can least absorb it.

## Why this keeps happening

Three reasons, none of them the rider's fault.

**The marketing is wrong on purpose.** "E-bike" is what sells. Listings advertise 28 mph as a feature in the same breath as calling the device an e-bike, and both statements can't be true in New York.

**The classes are genuinely confusing.** New York City permits Class 3 e-bikes at 25 mph, which most of the state doesn't. So "25 mph is fine here" is true — and gets misremembered as "fast is fine here."

**The 15 mph riding cap is a separate rule from all of this.** Since October 24, 2025, every e-bike in New York City is limited to **15 mph while riding**, regardless of class. That's a limit on your behaviour. The class rules are a limit on the machine. Getting one right doesn't protect you on the other.

## Before you buy anything

1. **Ask for the real top speed in writing.** If the seller won't put it in a message, that's your answer.
2. **Check for a UL 2849 mark on the frame and a UL 2271 mark on the battery.** Photograph them.
3. **Be sceptical of "28 mph" listed as a selling point.** In New York that's a warning label.
4. **Buying used from a marketplace? Assume nothing.** No dealer registration, no paperwork, no recourse.
5. **If you're already riding something you're unsure about**, the DMV can tell you whether a specific vehicle requires registration. It's a short conversation and much cheaper than finding out on the street.

## What this means if you deliver in NYC

Your bike is your income. A seizure isn't an inconvenience — it's the end of your earnings until you can find another $1,500.

The safe position is straightforward: a bike that stays within the e-bike definition, carries the UL marks, and came from someone who will still be reachable in six months.
