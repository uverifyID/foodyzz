# Laundromat Acquisition Playbook

> **Obeys** [`00-strategy/brand-source-of-truth.md`](../00-strategy/brand-source-of-truth.md). Invent nothing.
> UniHamper is **the marketplace, not the laundromat.** We bring shops qualified campus demand; the
> shop keeps 100% of its own rate. Never quote a specific $/lb — providers set their own prices.
> Tone: honest, specific, low-pressure. This is a trust business — no hype, no fake urgency.

---

## 0. The offer in one breath (memorize this)
> "UniHamper is a campus laundry marketplace launching **Aug 1, 2026**. We send you student wash-and-fold
> orders. **You set your own prices, and we take $0.00 commission** — you keep your full rate. Payouts
> run through Stripe twice a month, or daily for a $0.99 fee. Listing is free. If you can also do pickup
> and delivery, that's an extra **$9.99 flat per run** that goes straight to the driver."

### Why a laundromat says yes (the five real levers)
1. **$0.00 platform commission.** Not a discount — zero. You keep your full rate. (Competitors take a cut.)
2. **You set your own prices.** UniHamper never sets or caps your pricing.
3. **Free, qualified campus demand.** Students near you who need wash/fold — no ad spend on your end.
4. **Fast, clean payouts.** Stripe, twice monthly, or daily for a $0.99 admin fee. No chasing invoices.
5. **Optional extra income.** Offer pickup & delivery and earn **$9.99 flat per run**, paid to the driver.

### What we do NOT claim (guardrails)
- No promised order volume, revenue numbers, or "X students already signed up." We haven't launched.
- No specific launch-campus claims until Raj confirms the campus list.
- No competitor names in writing unless factual and neutral.
- Never say we set prices, take a percentage, or handle the washing. We don't.

**Opening (get permission):**
> "Hi, is the owner or manager around? I'm [Name] with UniHamper — I'll keep it to 60 seconds. Is now okay

**The hook:**
> "We're a campus laundry marketplace launching August 1. Students near here request wash-and-fold on their

> You set your prices and keep 100%. Listing is free."

**The ask:**
> "Would you be open to me setting you up with a free listing so you're ready when we go live? It takes a
> few minutes and there's no cost or commitment."

**If busy / "leave me info":**
> "Totally fair. Here's a card — the site's unihamper.com, and I'm on Instagram @unihamperhq. Can I grab the
> best email or number to send the signup link?"

**Close:**
> "Great — I'll send it today. One quick thing: do you currently offer pickup and delivery, or just in-shop
> wash and fold? That decides whether you also want the $9.99-per-run driver option."

## 5. Objection handling (say it plainly)

**"I already have plenty of customers."**
> "Makes sense — this isn't meant to replace your regulars. It's incremental student demand you're not
> marketing to, at $0 commission. If it's slow, you ignore it; if orders come in, it's your full rate. No
> downside to being listed."

**"What's the catch? Nobody's free."**
> "Fair question. We make money on the customer side and on the optional $9.99 delivery, not on your wash
> price. That's why owners keep 100%. No monthly fee, no exclusivity — you can leave anytime."

**"How and when do I get paid?"**
> "Through Stripe, straight to your bank. Standard is twice a month; if you'd rather get paid daily, that's
> a $0.99 admin fee per payout. You set that preference when you list."

**"Is this actually worth my time?"**
> "Setup is a few minutes and it's free, so the bar is low. You keep your full rate on every order, and you
> only do work when a paid order comes in. If it never sends you a single order, you've lost a few minutes.
> If it does, it's revenue you weren't getting."

**"Do you set my prices / take a percentage?"**
> "No — you set your own prices and we take $0.00 of your wash rate. That's the core of the whole thing."

**"Who does the pickup and delivery?"**
> "That's optional. If you have someone who can drive, they earn $9.99 flat per run. If not, no problem —
> you can be an in-shop wash-and-fold provider only."

## 6. "Sign your first 5 shops per campus" — step by step

Goal: **5 listed, launch-ready laundromats within pickup range of each target campus.** Five gives students
real choice and price competition from day one.

1. **Map the trade area.** Pull every laundromat / wash-and-fold within a realistic pickup radius of the
   campus (search maps for "laundromat" + "wash and fold near [campus]"). Aim for a list of 10–15 to work
   down to 5 signed.
2. **Rank by fit.** Prioritize shops that (a) already do wash-and-fold, (b) have decent reviews, (c) are
   closest to dorms. Star any that already advertise pickup/delivery — they're warmest for the $9.99 option.
3. **First touch, in person if you can.** Walk in with the 60-second script (§3). In-person converts best for
   local owners. If you can't visit, cold email Variation A (§1).
4. **Capture contact + preference on the spot.** Best email/number, and whether they do delivery. Log it.
5. **Send the signup link same day** (Touch 1, §4). Momentum matters — a warm "yes" goes cold in 48 hours.
6. **Work the follow-up sequence** (§4) for anyone who didn't finish signup.
7. **Confirm the listing is live and correct** — prices entered, hours right, Stripe payout preference set.
   A half-finished listing doesn't count toward your 5.
8. **Stop at 5 quality shops per campus**, then repeat on the next campus. Depth per campus beats spraying
   one shop across many campuses.

**Track it (simple sheet):** Shop name · Campus · Contact · Does delivery? (Y/N) · Touch # · Status
(Not started / Contacted / Signup sent / Listed-live) · Notes.
