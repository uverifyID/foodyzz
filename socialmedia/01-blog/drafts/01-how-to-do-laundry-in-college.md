# How to Do Laundry in College: The Complete Dorm Survival Guide

Nobody hands you a laundry manual at move-in. One week in, the hamper is a mountain, you're out of clean socks, and the dorm machines are somehow always full. Been there.

Good news: laundry is genuinely simple once someone shows you the five steps. This is that guide — no fluff, no lecture. By the end you'll know how to sort, wash, dry, and fold without turning a white tee pink. And if you're too slammed to deal with it at all, we'll cover the shortcut too.

## Before you start: the three-minute setup

Get these once and laundry stops being a scramble:

- **A hamper you can carry.** A backpack-style laundry bag beats a stiff basket when the machines are two floors down.
- **Detergent.** Pods are the easiest — one pod per normal load, no measuring, no spills. Liquid or powder works fine if you'd rather.
- **Quarters or your laundry card.** Check how your building's machines take payment *before* you're standing there with wet clothes.
- **A few dryer sheets** (optional, but they cut static and make everything smell clean).

That's it. You don't need fabric softener, stain kits, or a drying rack to survive freshman year.

## Step 1 — Sort your laundry

Sorting is the one step that saves you from a ruined wardrobe. Split your pile into three:

1. **Lights** — whites, pastels, light grays.
2. **Darks** — black, navy, red, and anything brand-new and brightly dyed (new stuff bleeds the most).
3. **Delicates or towels** — anything that says "hand wash," plus bulky towels and sheets, which are easier to run on their own.

If you're short on time and machines, at minimum keep **new dark colors away from whites**. That's the split that actually matters.

> **Quick answer people search for:** *How often should you do laundry in college?* For most students, **once a week** keeps you ahead of the pile. Two to three loads a week if you work out or play a sport. (We break this down in [How Often Should College Students Do Laundry?](/blog/how-often-should-college-students-do-laundry).)

## Step 2 — Load and start the washer

- **Don't overstuff.** Clothes need room to move. A good rule: fill the drum about three-quarters full, and you should be able to fit a flat hand on top of the pile.
- **Add detergent correctly.** For pods, toss it in the empty drum *first*, then your clothes on top. For liquid or powder, use the dispenser drawer.
- **Pick your settings.** When in doubt: **cold water, normal cycle.** Cold protects colors, saves energy, and handles everyday dirt fine. Save warm/hot for towels, sheets, and truly grimy gym gear.
- **Check pockets.** One forgotten pen or AirPod can wreck a whole load. Five-second scan, every time.

Start it, and set a timer on your phone for the cycle length (usually 25–35 minutes). Dorm etiquette rule number one: **don't leave your stuff in the machine after it's done.** People will pile your wet clothes on a table, and honestly, fair.

## Step 3 — Dry it right

- **Shake out each piece** as you move it to the dryer so nothing dries in a wrinkled ball.
- **Toss in a dryer sheet** if you have one.
- **Medium heat is your friend.** High heat is what shrinks that perfect hoodie and fades your favorite tee. Anything you care about, dry on low or medium.
- **Air-dry the fragile stuff.** Athletic wear, anything stretchy, and "hang to dry" labels do best draped over a chair or a cheap over-door rack.

Most loads take one full dryer cycle. If things are still damp, another 10–15 minutes beats cranking the heat higher.

## Step 4 — Fold (or at least don't let it wrinkle)

The trick to wrinkle-free clothes isn't ironing — it's **folding straight out of the dryer while everything's still warm.** Do it right there if you can. If you're carrying it back to your room, fold within a few minutes of getting there.

A dead-simple system:
- **T-shirts and tops:** fold in thirds, stack.
- **Jeans and pants:** fold in half lengthwise, then in half again.
- **Underwear and socks:** don't overthink it — a bin, done.
- **Anything that wrinkles (button-downs, dresses):** hang immediately.

Ten minutes of folding now saves you from steaming a shirt before class later.

## Step 5 — Build a routine so it never piles up

The students who never stress about laundry all do the same thing: they **pick a day and stick to it.** Sunday morning, Wednesday night — whatever fits your schedule. Machines are usually emptiest early morning or late at night, and never on Sunday afternoon when the whole floor has the same idea.

Keep a small "laundry-ready" stash — a spare set of everything — so a busy week never leaves you stranded in gym shorts to a 9 a.m.

## Common dorm laundry mistakes to skip

- **Washing everything on hot** — fades colors and shrinks favorites. Default to cold.
- **Overloading to save quarters** — clothes come out still dirty; you just re-wash later.
- **Leaving clothes in the machine** — the fastest way to annoy your entire floor.
- **Ignoring "hand wash" labels** — one hot cycle can destroy a nice sweater.
- **Letting the pile hit critical mass** — a weekly rhythm is easier than a four-load marathon.

## The five-step recap

1. **Sort** — lights, darks, delicates/towels.
2. **Wash** — cold water, normal cycle, don't overstuff, check pockets.
3. **Dry** — medium heat, air-dry the fragile stuff.
4. **Fold** — straight out of the dryer, while it's warm.
5. **Routine** — same day each week, off-peak hours.
