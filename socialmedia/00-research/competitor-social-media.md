# NYC E-Bike Rental Social Media Research Report

**Methodology note on confidence:** Instagram and TikTok block most automated fetches, so counts below come from a mix of (a) direct WebFetch renders of profile pages (more reliable, but Instagram sometimes serves cached/approximate numbers to non-logged-in fetchers) and (b) search-engine snippets (less reliable, sometimes stale or duplicated from aggregator sites). ## What's working well (cross-brand observations)

1. **JOCO has the most coherent, active social strategy** of the group — near-weekly posting, clear promo-code CTAs tied to city launches, and unusually for this category, cross-posting with a safety/advocacy org (Transportation Alternatives), which builds legitimacy with a workforce that's wary of corporate messaging.
2. **Whizz has the largest owned IG audience among active courier-focused brands** (~4.4-4.6K) and multi-city bio targeting (NYC/NJ/Philly/Chicago/DC/SF), suggesting more marketing investment than competitors, even if cadence is modest.
3. **Revel's IG scale (24.7K) proves the audience ceiling is much higher than anyone in the current courier-e-bike category has reached** — though that audience was built pre-pivot and isn't transferable evidence of courier-content appetite.

## Concrete content gaps (opportunity areas)

1. **No verified Spanish, Bengali, or Chinese-language social content from any of the seven brands**, despite NYC's own DOT explicitly localizing delivery-worker safety materials into English, Spanish, French, Bengali, and Chinese because the deliverista workforce is predominantly Spanish-speaking immigrants, plus significant Chinese and Bengali-speaking populations. This is a clear, sourced, unaddressed gap — every brand researched posts in English only as far as could be verified.
2. **Nobody is doing visible rider-earnings/tips content on social**, even though earnings-focused articles exist on brand blogs (Whizz's "Maximize Earnings & Success: New York Courier Strategies," "How much can you get in tips as a delivery driver"; Zoomo's "How to start work as a delivery courier"). This blog content isn't being repurposed into Reels/TikTok/Shorts — a low-effort, high-relevance content format nobody is capturing on social specifically.
3. **TikTok is broadly underdeveloped across the category** — Whizz (~259 followers), Fly E-Bike (~454), Revel (~125) are all negligible; Zoomo and Popwheels have no verifiable TikTok presence at all; only JOCO shows organic discovery-page activity suggesting real (possibly rider-generated/UGC) traction. Given TikTok's strength for authentic, worker-to-worker "day in the life" content, and that delivery workers themselves are prolific TikTok/Reels creators (the existence of "Joco Bike Reviews" and "Whizz Bike Nyc Review" discovery tags shows riders already make this content organically), no brand appears to be systematically amplifying/reposting or partnering with rider-creators.
4. **Popwheels has essentially zero organic social presence** (30-40 IG followers, 2 posts) despite being a funded, press-covered, delivery-worker-specific product (battery swap network) — a stark gap between PR/funding narrative and actual community-building with its target user base.
5. **No brand shows evidence of safety-training or battery-fire-safety content** on social, despite this being an intensely covered NYC policy topic (FDNY-approved charging cabinets, lithium battery fire prevention) that's highly relevant and shareable for this exact audience — JOCO's site mentions being "first mobility company in NYC to receive FDNY approval for battery charging cabinets" but this doesn't appear in sampled social content.
6. **Facebook presence is fragmented into many low-follower regional/store pages** (seen clearly with Zoomo and Fly E-Bike) rather than one consolidated page — diluting social proof and making it harder for a new market's customers to find the right account.
