# NYC Deliverista Market Brief — Research Findings

## 1. Minimum Pay Rate & Actual Earnings (2026)

**DCWP Minimum Pay Rate history** (per hour, excludes tips):
- Dec 2023 (enforcement began): **$17.96/hr**
- April 1, 2024: **$19.56/hr**
- April 1, 2025: **$21.44/hr** (final phase-in + 7.41% inflation adjustment)
- **April 1, 2026: $22.13/hr** — a 3.2% inflation adjustment (Dec 2024–Dec 2025 CPI). DCWP is required to announce the adjustment by Feb 1 each year.
- Pre-law baseline: workers averaged just **$5.39/hr before tips**.

Sources: [Jackson Lewis - Final Phase](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/final-phase-nyc-minimum-pay-rate-increase-app-based-delivery-workers-effect), [NYC.gov - Full Minimum Pay Rate](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/news/009-25/mayor-adams-full-minimum-pay-rate-app-based-restaurant-delivery-workers-now-in), [NYC.gov - Landmark Protections 2026](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/news/009-26/major-victory-nyc-delivery-workers-landmark-protections-take-effect-today)

**Actual take-home pay (DCWP quarterly reports):**
- As of a Sept 2025 DCWP quarterly report: deliveristas work an average of **17.4 hrs/week**, earning **$366.51/week** — annualizing to under $20,000/year. (Note: low weekly hours reflects that many work multiple apps/platforms, not necessarily part-time overall.)
- Combined hourly pay+tips rose from **$10.48/hr (pre-law) to $27.32/hr** — a 161% increase since Dec 2023 enforcement began.
- Average tip per delivery: rose from $1.18 to $2.29 under the new rules, though still below the $3.66 average tip seen in Nov 2023 before Uber Eats/DoorDash redesigned their apps to discourage tipping.
- Since Jan 26, 2026 tipping-protection enforcement began, tips have **doubled**; NYC's ~70,000 app-based workers are projected to earn an extra **$184 million annually in tips** (~$2,287/worker/year). A DCWP report also cites **$104 million** returned to workers in tips as of July 2026.
- Cornell/Los Deliveristas Unidos 2021 survey (pre-law, 500 couriers): average **$12.21/hr including tips**, base pay under $8/hr; other summaries cite "just over $12/hr after tips and expenses."

Sources: [NYC Mayor's Office - $100M+ July 2026](https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2026/07/mayor-mamdani-delivers-more-than--100-million-for-delivery-worke), [Documented NY - $104M in tips](https://documentedny.com/2026/07/29/nyc-delivery-workers-earned-millions-tips/), [DCWP Delivery App Report PDF](https://www.nyc.gov/content/dam/nycgov/nyc-main/pdf/2026/Delivery-App-Report.pdf), [LaborPress](https://www.laborpress.org/study-confirms-new-york-city-deliveristas-are-underpaid/)

## 2. Number of Delivery Workers

Estimates vary by source and date — **no single authoritative count**:
- **60,000** — commonly cited round figure across older reporting.
- **65,000+** app-based workers — cited in some 2024-era coverage.
- **70,000** — DCWP/Mayor's Office figure used in July 2026 press materials.
- **80,000** — DCWP figure cited in a January 26, 2026 announcement, and also used by CBS/Planetizen in April 2026 deliverista-hub coverage ("some of the city's 80,000 delivery workers").

Take the range as **60,000–80,000**, with **70,000–80,000** the more current (2026) DCWP-sourced figures. I could not verify a single reconciled official count — DCWP itself has used both 70,000 and 80,000 within the same year.

Sources: [NYC Mayor's Office July 2026](https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2026/07/mayor-mamdani-delivers-more-than--100-million-for-delivery-worke), [Planetizen - Deliverista Hub](https://www.planetizen.com/news/2026/04/137337-nyc-opens-nations-first-deliverista-hub), [NYC Food Policy Center](https://www.nycfoodpolicy.org/los-deliveristas-unidos-fights-for-equality-among-delivery-workers/)

## 3. Demographics

Primary source: **"Essential but Unprotected: App-Based Food Couriers in New York City"** (Sept 2021), by Workers' Justice Project / Los Deliveristas Unidos with Cornell ILR School's Worker Institute — survey of **500 app-based couriers** plus focus groups and interviews. Full PDF: https://andrewwolfsociology.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/los-deliveristas-unidos-report-2021.pdf (I could fetch search snippets/summaries referencing it but the PDF itself exceeded the fetch tool's size limit, so figures below come from secondary reporting on the report rather than a direct read of the source tables — flagging this as not independently verified against the primary document).

Reported figures (consistent across multiple secondary sources citing the report):
- **~80% of deliveristas are immigrants**; the other ~20% are described as mostly Black Americans.
- Countries/regions of origin cited: **Mexico, Guatemala, Bangladesh, Pakistan, West Africa, and China's Fujian province**.
- Other summaries describe the population as "disproportionately male, young, and immigrant," with a "significant majority identifying as Latino/a," most from Mexico or Guatemala.
- One source describes the workforce as ">90% people of color and immigrants."
- Languages: reporting mentions **Spanish, Bangla, and Mandarin** as dominant languages among the workforce, consistent with the origin countries above. I could not find a report with a precise language-share breakdown (e.g., "% Spanish speakers"), nor could I verify specific percentages for West African sub-groups (Guinean/Malian) or a Chinese/Fujianese percentage — these appear only as named-but-unquantified groups in every source I found.

Safety/conditions findings from the same 500-person survey:
- **42%** reported non-payment or underpayment of pay/tips.
- **49%** reported having been in an accident/crash while on a delivery; of those, **75%** paid medical costs out of pocket.
- **54%** reported experiencing e-bike theft; of those, **~30%** were physically assaulted during the robbery.

Caveat: I could not locate a report (Cornell, Comptroller, or DCWP) with a clean demographic percentage table (e.g., "42% Mexican, 18% Guatemalan, 15% West African, 12% Bangladeshi"). All figures found are qualitative/rounded ("most," "about 80%") rather than a precise breakdown by nationality — treat any more granular percentage claim as unverified.

Sources: [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2021/09/nyc-food-delivery-workers-face-harrowing-world), [The Counter](https://thecounter.org/new-yorks-deliveristas-cities-sustainable-transportation-bicycles/), [Inside Climate News](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/18102021/new-york-bicycle-food-delivery/), [Cornell ILR Worker Institute](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/worker-institute/blog/publications-0/first-its-kind-industry-report-shows-new-york-citys-app-based-delivery-workers-experience-harsh), [New Labor Forum](https://newlaborforum.cuny.edu/2023/05/10/los-deliveristas-unidos-and-the-ideals-of-worker-justice/)

## 4. E-Bikes: Purchase, Rental, Charging, Theft

**Purchase price:**
- Range cited: **$1,000–$3,000** for the e-bikes commonly used by couriers.
- One specific worker case: e-bike **~$1,800**, plus **~$580** for extra batteries (batteries last only 2-3 hours, so workers need 2+ to cover a shift).

**Rental / rent-to-own market (2026 pricing from vendor sites):**
- **Zoomo**: from **$20/week** for basic rental (Uber Eats-linked promo); standard plan **$59/week** ($49 base + $10 mandatory theft insurance); purchase/rent-to-own from **$45/month**.
- **Whizz**: rent-to-own from **$169/month**, owned outright after 12 months; includes unlimited battery swaps, free maintenance, GPS tracking.

**Battery charging costs:**
- Informal/underground economy dominates — Los Deliveristas Unidos notes there is **no formal city infrastructure** for safe charging/storage, so workers pay individuals or shops informally.
- One documented case: a worker pays **$50/month** to have someone charge his batteries in a basement near his work area.
- One estimate: combined annual cost of maintenance, charging, insurance, and battery replacement is **$450–$550/year** per worker (not broken out by category).
- I could not find a systematic bodega-charging fee schedule (e.g., "$X per charge") — only anecdotal/individual arrangements.

**Public charging infrastructure ("Street Deliverista Hubs"):**
- First announced Oct 2022 (Mayor Adams + Sen. Schumer) as "first-in-nation" hubs.
- After years of delay, the **first hub actually opened in April 2026** at City Hall Park (249 Broadway) — **40 battery-charging cabinets**, plus rest space, bike repair, and worker-support services (wage theft, app deactivation guidance).
- A second hub on Broadway (Tribeca) was under construction as of April 2026.
- Proposed future sites: Verdi Square, Fordham Heights (converting vacant newsstands).
- Scale problem: 40 charging cabinets at one hub vs. an estimated 70,000-80,000 workers citywide — infrastructure remains extremely limited relative to demand.

**Lithium-ion battery fires (FDNY data, citywide, not delivery-specific):**
- 2020: 44 fires / 23 injuries / 0 deaths
- 2021: 104 fires / 79 injuries / 4 deaths
- 2022: 220 fires / 147 injuries / 6 deaths
- 2023 (through July 3): 114 investigations / 74 injuries / **13 deaths**
- This trajectory (steep annual increase) is the direct policy driver behind Local Law 39's UL-certification mandate.

**E-bike theft:**
- Cornell/LDU 500-person survey: **54%** had experienced e-bike theft; ~30% of those were physically assaulted during the theft.
- Older 2018 Queens College/CUNY study of 153 delivery workers: **37% had been robbed**.
- A rough "50% of delivery workers fall victim to e-bike theft" estimate also circulates.
- Individual anecdote: one worker lost 6 e-bikes over 2 years, ~$10,000 in losses.
- Workers reportedly pool money informally to cover medical costs and bike replacement, and some track/recover stolen bikes via GPS themselves, sometimes leading to violent confrontations.

Sources: [Marketplace - charging/storing](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2022/09/20/nycs-delivery-drivers-depend-on-e-bikes-but-charging-and-storing-them-isnt-easy), [NYU Wagner Capstone - Bike Theft Mitigation](https://wagner.nyu.edu/education/capstone/projects/bike-theft-mitigation-for-nyc-delivery-workers), [Documented NY - E-bike theft organizing](https://documentedny.com/2021/03/25/delivery-workers-organize-to-fight-rampant-e-bike-theft/), [Columbia News Service](https://columbianewsservice.com/2024/03/27/immigrant-food-delivery-workers-struggle-with-e-bike-thefts/), [TechCrunch - lithium battery fires](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/14/nyc-gig-workers-need-help-accessing-safe-e-bikes-amid-lithium-battery-fires/amp/), [Tribeca Citizen - hub construction](https://tribecacitizen.com/2026/04/02/deliverista-hub-on-broadway-is-under-construction/), [The City - Schumer/Mamdani hub opening](https://www.thecity.nyc/2026/04/07/deliverista-bike-hub-city-hall-schumer-mamdani/), [CBS NY - hub opens](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/deliverista-hub-city-all-new-york-city/), [Zoomo NYC](https://www.ridezoomo.com/us/home), [Whizz](https://www.getwhizz.com/electric-bike-rental-nyc)

## 5. NYC E-Bike Regulations (2026)

**Speed limit:**
- A citywide **15 mph speed cap** on e-bikes/e-scooters (streets, bike lanes, greenways, bridges, park drives) took effect **October 24, 2025**, regardless of a device's top mechanical speed. This is now in force, not merely proposed.

**UL certification (Local Law 39 of 2023):**
- Effective Sept 16, 2023, bars sale, lease, or rental in NYC of e-bikes/e-scooters/lithium batteries unless UL-certified: **UL 2849** (e-bike systems), **UL 2272** (e-scooters), **UL 2271** (lithium-ion batteries). This is the direct regulatory response to the battery-fire death toll above.

**Moped vs. e-bike classification (major enforcement issue):**
- Most fat-tire, throttle-only e-bikes capable of ~28 mph sold to delivery riders legally count as **mopeds**, not e-bikes, under NY State law — riding one without moped registration/license/insurance is illegal.
- NYC enforcement seized **27,000+ motorized vehicles in 2024** under this classification crackdown.
- NYPD began issuing criminal summonses for e-bike/moped traffic violations starting late April 2025; this policy was **rescinded March 27, 2026** by the Mamdani administration ("end to criminal enforcement for minor traffic [violations]").

**Department of Sustainable Delivery (DSD):**
- First announced Jan 2024, formally launched **July 2025** under DOT, after delays from Council/Adams friction.
- Mandate: enforcement against illegal moped/e-bike/e-scooter riding; holding delivery apps accountable for equipment legality and rider safety; addressing dangerous parking.
- FY2026 budget funds up to **45 new DOT peace officers** for commercial-cycling enforcement — but the **first class isn't deployed until 2028**, meaning enforcement capacity in 2026 remains minimal despite the department existing on paper.
- Adams-era proposal (unclear if finalized): require delivery apps to register with DOT and issue **unique ID numbers** to workers, worn on vests — worker advocates have raised surveillance/immigration-data-exposure concerns; Council criticized bypassing public input.

**Pending City Council legislation (as of an Aug 6, 2026 Streetsblog roundup of ~16 bills Speaker Julie Menin is weighing):**
- **Intro 950** (Schulman): requires delivery services to obtain a DCWP business license.
- **Intro 244** (Hudson): bans sale/rental of Class 3 e-bikes (25 mph throttle-capable).
- **Intro 389** (Restler): requires delivery apps to ensure mopeds are DMV-registered.
- **Intro 110** (Brooks-Powers): requires businesses to keep delivery-worker rosters, provide safety equipment/ID.
- **Intro 78** (Brewer): requires apps to disclose driver ID/location/timing data.
- **Reso 43** (Brewer): urges the state to create a commercial e-bike delivery registration program.
- Additional teased-but-undetailed items: a broader e-bike rider registration/licensing resolution, added insurance requirements for apps, trade-in incentives for unsafe devices.
- None of these were confirmed as passed/law as of this research — they are pending/under consideration.
