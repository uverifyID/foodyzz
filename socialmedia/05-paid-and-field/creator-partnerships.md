# Creator Partnerships & Community Alliances

> The biggest unclaimed opportunity in this category. Rider-creators already make e-bike rental content organically — discovery tags like "Joco Bike Reviews" and "Whizz Bike Nyc Review" exist with real traction — and **no brand is amplifying, reposting or partnering with any of them.**

---

## Why creators, and why now

Three facts make this the highest-leverage paid channel available:

1. **The category's own accounts are tiny.** Whizz ~4.5K on Instagram is the leader; TikTok is under 500 followers per brand. A single rider-creator with 20K followers has more reach than the entire competitive set combined.
2. **This audience trusts riders, not brands.** 42% report being underpaid by the platforms they work for. A company saying "we're honest" is worth very little; a rider they already follow saying "I checked the receipt, it's what they said" is worth a great deal.
3. **Nobody is bidding for these creators yet.** Rates will never be lower than they are right now.

---

## Who to look for

Search TikTok, Instagram and YouTube for: `nyc delivery`, `deliverista`, `doordash nyc`, `ubereats nyc`, `nyc ebike`, `repartidor nueva york`, `livreur new york`.

**Note: specific handles are deliberately not listed here.** Follower counts and account activity change weekly, and a stale list is worse than none. Build the list live, in a shared sheet, with these columns:

| Handle | Platform | Followers | Language | Content style | Posts/week | Comment quality | Contact | Rate | Status |

**Tiers to target:**

| Tier | Followers | What they're for | Typical ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nano** | 1K–10K | Volume. The best ROI in this category. | A free rental month, or $100–300 |
| **Micro** | 10K–50K | Reach with credibility intact | $300–1,000/post |
| **Mid** | 50K–200K | Awareness spikes | $1,000–3,000 |

**Start with nano and micro.** At this budget, ten nano creators beat one mid creator — more content, more languages, more neighbourhoods, and far less risk concentrated in one relationship.

**Prioritise Spanish and French speakers.** French is 46.5% of this workforce and no competitor has ever run a French creator campaign. That is a genuinely open field.

## The offers

### Offer A — The Honest Review *(default)*

Give a creator a bike for a month. Ask them to review it honestly, including what they don't like.

**Why the honesty clause is not a gimmick:** a review that lists a real drawback is more persuasive than one that doesn't, and this audience can smell a script. The brief should explicitly say "tell them what's missing — we don't do battery swaps, say so."

**Brief:** film the delivery, the first ride, the receipt screen, and a two-week check-in. Disclose the partnership. No script beyond the beats.

### Offer B — The Receipt Check

The sharpest format for this brand. A creator books a rental on camera and reads out every line of the checkout against what our marketing says.

If our numbers are right, this is the most convincing content that could possibly exist for a price-transparency position. **It only works if we're actually right — which is why the fact-checking discipline in the source-of-truth file matters commercially, not just legally.**

### Offer C — Affiliate

A code that gives the rider a discount and pays the creator per completed order. Ongoing, self-sustaining, and it aligns incentives toward customers who actually stay.

⚠️ **Blocked.** The app has a promo-code system on the Explore feed, but the referral fields (`referredByManagerId`, `referralCodeUsed`) exist on the user type with **nothing reading or writing them in the customer app**, and a code comment notes referral is "visibility / thank-you only — no commission." Building a real affiliate programme is a product change, not a marketing decision. See `../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` Q12.

### Offer D — Rules explainers

Pay a creator to explain the moped rule, the 15 mph cap, or what a 2026 summons means — in their own language, with a light Foodyzz credit. Public-service content that builds enormous goodwill and travels far.

## Rules for every partnership

1. **Disclosure, always.** `#ad` or `#sponsored`, and verbally in video. FTC requires it; more importantly, this audience punishes undisclosed ads brutally and permanently.
2. **Never buy a positive review.** Buy the review; let the verdict be theirs.
3. **Never ask for a script read.** Give facts and beats. Their voice is what you're paying for.
4. **Pay properly and pay fast.** These are working riders. Late payment from a company selling them financial products is a story that will get told.
5. **Contract it:** deliverables, usage rights (you'll want to run their content as Spark Ads), exclusivity window, and payment terms.
6. **Never ask anyone to film something unsafe** — no speed content, no helmetless riding, no traffic weaving. Put this in writing in the brief.

## Community alliances — different from creators, and more valuable

### Los Deliveristas Unidos / Workers' Justice Project

The organising body for this workforce. **Approach as a partner, not an advertiser.** Ask what they need before offering anything. Possible real contributions: sponsoring safety materials, funding a legal-aid clinic, providing bikes at cost for a programme, translating the rules content into the languages they serve.

**Do not pitch a marketing partnership in the first conversation.** They exist because this workforce gets exploited by companies with products to sell. Earn it.

### Documented NY

An immigrant-focused NYC newsroom publishing in English, Spanish, and Chinese, with a **dedicated WeChat official account** built precisely because that's the only way to reach Chinese-speaking readers. Pitch the rules content as a story, not an ad. Their Chinese-language reporting on e-bike rental contracts shows they already cover this exact subject.

### HungryPanda (熊猫外卖)

**The single highest-leverage BD conversation available.** NYC's Chinese-speaking riders largely work HungryPanda rather than DoorDash or Uber Eats, and **Zoomo already proved the playbook** — their only Chinese-language page is a HungryPanda partnership landing page, with no general Chinese site and no language switcher. They bought that audience through business development, not content.

That door is not exclusive. A partner rate for HungryPanda riders reaches a segment that is structurally unreachable through Google, Meta or TikTok.

### PopWheels

**Not a competitor — the natural complement.** They solve charging; we solve the bike. A rider on a Foodyzz bike still has to charge it, and we offer no swap network. Co-marketing costs neither party anything: a Foodyzz customer gets a PopWheels intro, a PopWheels customer sees Foodyzz at the cabinet. Their social presence is negligible, so there's no brand-conflict risk.

### Chinese-language classifieds

Get listed on **nychinaren.com** (纽约华人资讯网) and **usahuarenjie.com** (纽约华人街). This is where NYC Chinese-language listings actually live — not Google. Cheap, and the only realistic organic route to that segment.

### Community organisations by language

- **French / West African** — 46.5% of the workforce, least served by anyone. Community centres, mosques, and West African business associations.
- **Bengali** — JBBA in Jackson Heights, mosques in Kensington. **Physical and print, not SEO** — Bengali search volume for this is effectively zero because Bangladeshi riders in NYC search in English.

## Budget

**Month 1: $1,000–1,500** on creators, alongside the $1,500–2,500 paid media budget.

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| 5 nano creators, Offer A | $500–1,000 |
| 2 micro creators, Offer B | $600–1,500 |
| Rules explainer content, 2 creators | $400 |

**Success measure:** cost per attributed install, and — more importantly — whether the content is good enough to run as a Spark Ad. A creator video that outperforms our own creative as a paid ad has paid for itself several times over.
