# Field Sales Playbook

> **Reconciled against the live product on Aug 7, 2026.** Pricing, the Protection Plan, the single bike model and the 15 mph limit all changed that day — see `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` Q3, Q4b, Q6 and Q16.


> **This is where the first fifty customers come from.** Not from ads, not from SEO. From standing where riders stand and talking to them.
>
> The evidence backs this: NYSERDA's researchers could only reach this workforce by distributing their survey through Los Deliveristas Unidos' **WhatsApp group**. Chinese-language reporting says riders find rental shops **by word of mouth**. This is a referral market with a physical layer, and it rewards showing up.

## Why field work beats everything else at the start

| | Field | Paid social | SEO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost per conversation | Your time | $15–40 CPM, unproven | $0 marginal |
| Time to first customer | Same day | 1–2 weeks | 3–6 months |
| Trust transfer | Highest — they meet a person | Lowest | Medium |
| Language barrier | Solvable in the moment | Hard | Hard |
| Learning per interaction | Enormous | Minimal | None |

The last row matters most in week one. Fifty conversations at the Deliverista Hub will teach you more about objections, pricing sensitivity and language mix than any amount of analytics.

## The locations

### Tier 1 — go here first, go here often

**Deliverista Hub · 249 Broadway, City Hall Park**
The single best location in New York for this. Opened April 2026, first in the nation, **40 battery-charging cabinets**, rest space, bike repair, and worker support services. Open 24/7. Riders are **sitting still, waiting for a battery, with time to talk** — which is the rarest condition in this job.

*Etiquette matters here.* It is a public facility built for these workers, not a lead-gen venue. Do not block cabinets. Do not interrupt someone eating. Ask before you sit down. If the site has rules about commercial activity, follow them and ask staff first — being asked to leave the best location in the city would be an expensive mistake.

**Best hours:** 2–5pm (between lunch and dinner rush) and after 9pm.

**PopWheels swap cabinets — ~30 sites around Manhattan**
Same logic, smaller crowds, more of them. Riders swapping a battery have 2–3 minutes standing still.

**DOT charging pilot sites:** Cooper Square, Essex Market, Brooklyn Army Terminal, Plaza de las Américas, Willoughby/Jay St.

### Tier 2 — restaurant clusters at shift change

Riders queue outside busy restaurants waiting for orders. Dense, repeat, and they're already talking to each other — which is how word of mouth starts.

Work the clusters in the current service area: **Upper West Side** (Broadway in the 70s–90s), **Harlem** (125th St), **Chelsea** (8th Ave), **SoHo** (Broadway/Spring). These are the neighbourhoods Foodyzz actually delivers to, so the conversation ends in a booking rather than a "we don't serve you yet."

**Best hours:** 5:30–8:30pm, every day.

### Tier 3 — community anchors

- **Los Deliveristas Unidos / Workers' Justice Project** — the organising body for this workforce. Approach as a partner, not an advertiser. Ask what they need; offer safety content, offer a discount for members, offer to sponsor something real. **Do not pitch them a marketing partnership in the first conversation.**
- **Jackson Heights** — Bangladeshi community, JBBA (Jackson Heights Bangladeshi Business Association). Bengali flyers, not Bengali SEO.
- **Kensington** — mosques and Bangladeshi community centres.
- **Flushing and Sunset Park** — Chinese-speaking riders, largely working HungryPanda. NYSERDA found Sunset Park is a top rider home-ZIP cluster.
- **West African community centres** — this is 46.5% of the workforce and the least-served by every competitor. French-language materials, and a French speaker doing the talking, is a genuine unfair advantage.

## The kit

- **Flyers, double-sided, in English, Spanish and French.** Front: the price table and "Own it in 12 months — $999.00." Back: a QR code and the four things we need to rent.
- **A unique QR per location** pointing at `foodyzz.com/r/[location]` so you can measure which spots convert. This is the difference between field work and wandering.
- **The demo bike.** Nothing sells like letting someone sit on it and feel the weight.
- **A phone with the app open** to the itemised checkout screen.
- **A charged battery in your hand** to demonstrate the removal.

## The conversation

**Opening — do not open with the product.**

> "You riding for DoorDash or Uber? … How's the bike holding up?"

Every rider has a bike opinion. Let them talk. You'll learn whether they own, rent, or borrow, and whether they've been burned — which determines everything that follows.

**The pivot, once you know their situation:**

| Their situation | What to lead with |
|---|---|
| Renting from a competitor | "What are you actually paying, all in?" Then show the receipt screen. |
| Owns a bike, worried about it | The moped question. "How fast does it go?" If ~28, they have a serious problem and you can help. |
| Bike was stolen | "How soon do you need to be back out? We can have one to you tomorrow evening." |
| New to delivering | Rent. Don't upsell a first-week rider into an 8-month commitment. |
| Been doing it 2+ years | Rent-to-buy. $999.00 and it's theirs. |
| Has cash | $999 outright, no documents, no deposit. |

**Close:** don't ask for a download on the spot — it's a big ask on a street corner with 4% battery. Ask them to **scan the QR**, which opens a page they can come back to. Then give them the flyer.

**Always leave two flyers.** One for them, one for the rider they'll talk to tonight.

## Facebook Groups

This is the online half of the same job, and it has rules that will get you banned if you break them.

**How to do it:**
1. Join as **a person** with a real name and a real face. Never as the Page.
2. **Spend two weeks answering questions before mentioning Foodyzz once.** Answer the rules questions — moped classification, the 15 mph cap, what a summons means now, how to read a certification mark — cTUVus, UL or ETL, and what each one means. That's real expertise most members don't have and it costs nothing to give away.
3. Mention the product only when somebody asks what to rent — and **disclose the affiliation every single time.** "Full disclosure, I work for Foodyzz, so take this with that in mind."
4. **Never post identical text to multiple groups.** Facebook flags it and admins notice.
5. When you get a hostile reply, answer it with a number and move on. Never argue.

**What to look for:** NYC delivery worker groups, deliverista groups, e-bike buy/sell groups, borough-specific rider groups, and Spanish-language equivalents. Search Facebook for "deliverista," "NYC delivery drivers," "e-bike NYC," and the Spanish terms. Group names and sizes change constantly — **build the list by searching, and log what you find in a shared sheet.**

**WhatsApp** is where the real conversation happens, but it's invitation-based. You don't join those groups by asking; you get added because a rider you helped adds you. That's a month-three outcome of doing month-one work properly.

## Measurement

Log every session. Minimum fields:

| Date | Location | Hours | Conversations | QR scans | Flyers | Signups within 72h | Orders within 7d | Notes |

**Kill any location that hasn't produced an order by week three.** Double down on the ones that have.

**The notes column is the most valuable thing here.** Write down objections verbatim. Those become caption copy, blog headlines and TikTok hooks. The "Real question, real number" series in `../03-social/tiktok-scripts.md` is fed entirely by this column.

**Target for week one:** 60 conversations, 25 QR scans, 5 signups, 2 orders. That's a realistic day-one-to-day-seven yield for one person working three sessions.

## Rules

1. **Never disparage a competitor to a rider's face.** If they're on a Whizz bike and happy, say "good bike" and move on. The market is 70,000 people.
2. **Never promise something the product doesn't do** — no battery swaps, no roadside help, no helmet supplied. On theft: the rental waiver caps what you owe at $500 on conditions; it is not theft insurance and it does not apply to Rent to Buy.
3. **Never give legal advice.** You can explain the rules and point at the source. You cannot tell someone what to do about their summons.
4. **Pay anyone who helps you** — translators, riders who introduce you, community connectors.
5. **If a rider says no, say thanks and leave.** They're working. Their time is money in the most literal sense.
6. **Follow the venue's rules.** Getting banned from the Deliverista Hub would cost more than any campaign.
