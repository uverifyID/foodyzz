# Channel Playbooks — Instagram, Facebook, TikTok

> How each platform is used, why, and what "good" looks like. Read `../01-strategy/positioning-and-messaging.md` for voice first.
>
> **The category context that shapes all three:** the market leader has ~4.5K Instagram followers, and the entire category's TikTok presence is under 500 followers per brand. Revel — a pivoted-away moped company — has 24.7K followers, which proves the ceiling is far higher than anyone has reached. **This is an under-marketed category, not a saturated one.** Volume and consistency beat polish.

---

## TikTok — the priority channel

**Why first:** no incumbent. Whizz ~259 followers, Fly E-Bike ~454, Revel ~125. Zoomo and PopWheels have no confirmed account at all. Meanwhile riders already make this content organically — discovery tags like "Joco Bike Reviews" and "Whizz Bike Nyc Review" exist with real view counts and **no brand is amplifying or partnering with any of them.**

**Cadence:** 6/week. This is the number, not an aspiration. In a category this empty, the algorithm rewards volume more than production value.

**Format rules**
- Vertical 9:16, filmed on a phone. **Do not make these look like ads.** Polished corporate video underperforms badly with this audience.
- Hook in the first 2 seconds, and the hook must be a claim or a number, not a greeting.
- On-screen text always — most viewing is sound-off, and a meaningful share of the audience reads English better than they hear it.
- 20–45 seconds for explainers, 8–15 for a single fact.
- Real streets, real bike, real hands. No stock.

**Content mix**
| Share | Type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 40% | Rules & risk explainers | "Your bike does 28. That's not an e-bike, that's a moped." |
| 25% | Price transparency | "Here's every line on a Foodyzz checkout." |
| 20% | Product / spec | Certification mark on the frame, close up. |
| 10% | Rider stories (once we have customers) | |
| 5% | Behind the scenes | Delivery runs, staging bikes. |

**Never on TikTok:** speed content, riding without a helmet on camera, weaving through traffic, police-evasion framing, anything that could be read as encouraging illegal riding. It's off-brand, it's unsafe, and it invites exactly the wrong regulatory attention on a company that rents e-bikes in this city.

**Comments are the channel.** Answer every price question with the actual number. This is where trust gets built in public, and it's free.

## Instagram — the credibility channel

**Why:** this is where a rider checks whether you're real before installing an app. Whizz's ~4.5K following is the category benchmark and it's beatable.

**Cadence:** Reels ×2/week, carousel ×2/week, Stories ×3/week.

**Format rules**
- **Reels:** repurpose TikToks, but re-upload natively without a TikTok watermark — watermarked reposts get suppressed.
- **Carousels:** the workhorse. Slide 1 is a claim, slides 2–6 are the proof, final slide is the CTA. Carousels are where the price table and the comparison tables live.
- **Stories:** delivery-day content, polls, question stickers, link stickers to the blog.
- **Highlights, from day one:** `Prices` · `The Bikes` · `NYC Rules` · `How It Works` · `FAQ`. A profile with organised highlights reads as a real business; one without reads as a dropshipper.

**Grid discipline:** brand green `#86B54F` as fill with black text, the neubrutalist hard-shadow style, JetBrains Mono for numbers. See `../04-visuals/graphic-templates.md`.

**Bio:** see `../01-strategy/gtm-launch-plan.md` §3.

## Facebook — the distribution channel

**Why:** this is where the audience actually organises. NYSERDA's researchers had to distribute their survey through Los Deliveristas Unidos' **WhatsApp group** to reach this workforce; Facebook Groups and Marketplace are where used e-bikes change hands. Facebook is not a vanity channel here — it's the closest thing to the community's town square.

**Two distinct jobs, don't confuse them:**

**1. The Page** — 3 posts/week. Longer-form than Instagram. Link posts to the blog perform fine here, unlike on IG. This is also where Meta ads run from and where the pixel audience builds.

**2. Groups** — this is the higher-yield half, and it has rules.

**How to be in Groups without getting banned or hated**
- Join as **a person**, not as the Page. Use a real staff account with a real face.
- **Spend two weeks answering questions before mentioning Foodyzz once.** This audience has excellent instincts for being sold to and Group admins ban brand accounts on sight.
- Answer the rules questions — moped classification, 15 mph, summonses, certification marks. That's genuine expertise we have and most Group members don't.
- Mention the product only when someone asks what to rent, and disclose the affiliation every single time.
- **Never post the same message to multiple groups.** Facebook flags it and admins notice.

**Language:** Spanish-language groups matter as much as English ones. See `../05-paid-and-field/field-sales-playbook.md` for the specific groups to find and how to approach them.

## The language question — the biggest strategic call on this page

NYSERDA's 2025 intercept survey of 331 NYC delivery workers found the primary-language split:

| Language | Share |
|---|---|
| **French** (West African) | **46.5%** |
| Spanish | 25.1% |
| English | 21.8% |
| Chinese | 6.6% |

**No competitor posts social content in any language but English.** Whizz and Zoomo both built French and Spanish *websites* — meaning they saw the same data and acted on it for SEO, but left social untouched.

**Recommendation:** caption every post in **English + Spanish** from day one, and add **French** as soon as there's someone who can write it properly. Machine translation is worse than nothing with this audience — it reads as exactly what it is.

Two caveats worth respecting:
- Many West African riders speak French as a *second* language alongside Wolof, Fula, Mandinka or Arabic, and literacy varies. French text helps, but field presence and word of mouth matter more.
- Chinese-speaking riders are structurally off Google and off Western social — their information layer is **WeChat**, and they largely work **HungryPanda** rather than DoorDash or Uber Eats. Reach them through partnership and physical presence in Flushing and Sunset Park, not through posting.

## Cross-posting rules

| From | To | How |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok | IG Reels | Re-upload the source file. Never the watermarked export. |
| TikTok | YouTube Shorts | Straight repost, fine. |
| IG carousel | FB Page | Works as-is. |
| Blog post | All three | Via the Blotato pipeline — `../06-automation/blotato-pipeline.md` |
| **Anything** | **Twitter/X, LinkedIn** | **Don't.** The audience isn't there. Ignore both. |

## What we measure

**Weekly:** followers, reach, profile visits, link clicks, saves and shares (worth more than likes here), comment volume, DM volume.

**Monthly:** app installs attributed by platform, signups, and **paid orders** — the only number that decides whether any of this worked.

**The leading indicator to watch is DMs and comments asking about price.** In this category, a rider who asks "how much really?" in public is worth more than a hundred passive followers, because answering them publicly with a real number is the entire positioning demonstrated in front of an audience.
